# Daily English News

## Data flows

`/app/news` reads the RLS-protected `news_archive_catalog` view with database pagination. Users can search, filter by category, source, year and on-site readability, sort newest or oldest, save a headline, and open the publisher's original page. The view joins article metadata to a rights-controlled body and a separately reviewed preview image when available. Preview images also appear on the detail and saved-news pages. Imported rows are retained for historical browsing; the RSS feed's own archive depth and how far back it has been ingested determine actual coverage.

For the official NASA feeds (News Releases, Technology, Aeronautics, Space Station) and NOAA feeds, the sync also extracts plain text from individually eligible entries into `news_article_bodies`. NASA entries qualify when they link to `www.nasa.gov` or `science.nasa.gov`. APOD entries are excluded because outside contributors write and credit them. NASA's template panels (author card, share/details, "Explore More", topic cards) and press-contact lines are dropped. NOAA Ocean Service RSS carries only a teaser, so the sync reads up to four article pages per run from `oceanservice.noaa.gov/news/`, newest first. `news_claim_body_fetch` records each attempt; a page without usable text waits seven days. Separately, approved NASA and NOAA feeds can supply one agency-hosted lead image per item to `news_article_images`, including metadata-only items. NASA and NOAA news images require an explicit agency credit in the feed. NOAA National Ocean Service images use its [published image guidance](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/about/faq.html): most are public domain unless otherwise credited, and the sync rejects third-party credits. Each stored image keeps its original URL, alt text, credit, and rights basis; the site links to the original article. Other images, embedded media, scripts, and entries bearing third-party copyright markers are excluded. The full-text rights basis cites [NASA's media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) or [NOAA's content guidance](https://www.noaa.gov/office-education/outreach-communication/faq). An image never implies that its article text can be republished. Admins can remove a body or preview if its rights status changes.

`/app/news/learn` reads original or specifically licensed material from `news_learning_articles`. Seven original sample passages cover the seven existing stages. Vocabulary lives in `news_vocabulary`, and quiz keys live separately in `news_answer_keys`. `news_submit_quiz` grades in PostgreSQL, saves attempts, awards XP once per article, and completes a linked planner task. `news_mark_read` and `news_finish_read` distinguish opening and finishing a passage. Words saved to the notebook are available in the Vocabulary Lab review deck. No AI service is used.

`/app/news/learn` also offers deterministic personal suggestions. Learners save a level and preferred topics in `user_news_preferences`. The order also considers completed readings, the most recent quiz result, and words saved in the notebook. The complete reading catalog and manual filters remain available.

The Daily News page presents one archive with an explicit on-site reading filter, separate from editor-authored lessons. A learner can mark an eligible full-text article as read once; `user_news_article_reads` stores the completion per account and the History page shows it. The admin dashboard explains that ordinary news arrives through Cron. Editors only need to add content when they want a curated quiz with fixed answer keys or when reviewing a new source's rights.

The full-text reader fills the available workspace and includes a per-article word explorer. Selecting one word looks up its English definition through [Free Dictionary API](https://dictionaryapi.dev/) and requests a Vietnamese machine translation. Once a meaning is available, the word is saved to the learner's notebook with the article path; the learner can edit the meaning. Bilingual mode aligns each English paragraph with Vietnamese. Chrome desktop uses its [built-in Translator API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/translator-api) on the learner's device. Other browsers use the server route only when `GOOGLE_CLOUD_TRANSLATE_API_KEY` is configured; without it, the reader shows a clear unavailable message and still allows a manual Vietnamese meaning. The fallback uses [Cloud Translation Basic](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/reference/rest/v2/translate) and is capped at 30,000 characters per account per day. Configure the API key only on the server, restrict it to the Translation API, and set a project quota before enabling it.

`/admin/news` is available only to an admin account. The admin can configure feed sources, review imported headlines, edit original/licensed readings, author vocabulary and answer-key questions, publish readings, and inspect sync logs. Database constraints require a recorded rights basis before enabling source ingestion and require a word plus a graded question before publishing a reading.

### Readable-only archive

Migration `202610250019_news_readable_only.sql` keeps only articles that can be read on site. A feed that carries text (NASA, NOAA News, Global Voices) stores just the items whose text passed the rights checks. A teaser-only feed (GOV.UK, NOAA Ocean Service) keeps an item only until its page has been read. If the page gives no usable text, `news_prune_unreadable()` removes the item at the end of each sync. Removed links are recorded in `news_skipped_urls`, so later syncs do not import them again. Articles linked to a Learn with News lesson and manual items are never pruned. The learner archive lists readable articles only. The NSF feed is disabled because it has no reusable text. The first prune on 2026-10-25 removed 343 headline-only rows. 472 GOV.UK and Ocean Service items remain queued until their pages are read.

### Vietnamese news written by AI

Vietnamese publishers keep their rights, so their articles are never copied. Migration `202610250020_news_ai_vietnam_rewrites.sql` adds four `ai_rewrite` sources: VnExpress International (news, life, business) and Tuoi Tre News. Each enrichment run, `src/lib/news/rewrite.ts` collects all four feeds and picks the **hottest unseen story of the last 24 hours**, favouring topics young readers follow. Choosing a story uses no AI; `rankStories` scores it as follows:

- **Hot:** signals read from both homepages: VnExpress "Most read" rank (60 points for #1, 4 fewer per place), top story (30), Trending (25), "Most liked" (20) and homepage position (up to 20). A story reported by both outlets, sharing three or more title words, gets 20 more.
- **Youth:** up to 40 points for matching topics (study, exams, jobs, technology, social media, music and concerts, film, sport, travel, food, fashion, mental health and similar), plus 10 for VnExpress life, tech, travel and sports sections.
- **Freshness:** minus 2 points per hour of age.

Stories without any hot signal are not written. Titles about politics, crime, death, accidents, rescues, podcasts or videos are dropped before any AI request. The writer is told its readers are about 16–30 and to keep the article engaging without changing any fact. It reads the report's page only as fact evidence (never stored) and then:

1. **Writer request.** Gemini writes an original English article of 330–420 words. Each sentence cites an exact quote from the source. It may mark the story unsuitable (politics, crime, death, disasters with victims, or too little detail).
2. **Code checks** (`verifyRewrite`):
   - At least 85% of each quote's word pairs occur in the source.
   - Every number, number word and name in a sentence appears in the source; a name may also share its stem, as in Vietnam and Vietnamese.
   - Uncited background sentences contain no names or numbers, and there are at most 3 of them or a quarter of the article.
   - There are no direct quotations and no 15-word run copied from the source.
   - The length is 280–520 words. The writer is asked for 5–6 paragraphs of 3–4 sentences.

   A failed draft gets one revision that is told the reason.
3. **Fact-checker request.** A second Gemini call, at temperature 0, labels every sentence SUPPORTED, BACKGROUND, UNSUPPORTED or CONTRADICTED. Any of the last two rejects the article.
4. **Quiz request.** The usual 20-question IELTS set is written from the verified article alone. After a revision, the regular quiz worker writes it in a later run.

Accepted articles are stored with `ai_generated = true` and a byline naming the source. The reader shows a warning label with a link to the original report. Rejected stories are recorded in `news_skipped_urls` (reason `ai_rewrite`) and never retried; a quota error leaves the story eligible. `daily_rewrite_limit` in `/admin/news/settings` (default 6; 0 disables it) caps attempts per day. Each attempt costs three Gemini requests, four with a revision. A live test on 2026-10-25 accepted a 382-word article about BigBang concert resale prices with 20 questions in 20 seconds. Earlier drafts were rightly rejected for copied wording, numbers that did not match their quote, and one story about deaths.

## Providers and rights

- GDELT DOC API: public article-list discovery, English source-language filter, metadata only. This source is paused after repeated production timeouts; its existing rows are retained. The publisher's full article is never fetched.
- NASA News Releases RSS: official feed at `https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/feed/`, initially approved for automatic metadata publication. Text from individually eligible NASA-authored items on `www.nasa.gov` and `science.nasa.gov` is shown on site with NASA attribution. A title that matches the sensitive-content filter still enters review.
- NOAA RSS: official feed at `https://www.noaa.gov/rss.xml`, approved for metadata publication. Individually eligible NOAA-authored text from `www.noaa.gov` news pages is shown on site with NOAA attribution. The feed may include third-party material, which the text extractor excludes when detected.
- NASA Technology, Aeronautics and Space Station: [official NASA RSS feeds](https://www.nasa.gov/rss-feeds/) with metadata, original links and the same NASA-authored full-text rule as News Releases. NASA News Releases additionally reads numbered historical RSS pages; `archive_next_page` advances two pages per successful scheduled sync. The authenticated admin can seed up to five selected pages per request through `POST /api/news/sync` with `{ "archivePages": [2, 3] }`.
- NOAA National Ocean Service: [official news RSS](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/rss.html) includes historical entries. Its unusual nested RSS entries are flattened during import. Eligible agency-hosted feed images appear as previews, and article text is read from the NOS page itself (see above). The [NSF news RSS](https://www.nsf.gov/rss) stores metadata and original links only.
- GOV.UK (migration `202610250018_news_open_licence_sources.sql`): five topic feeds (Health, Environment, Education, Science and Technology, Business), each an Atom search feed limited to selected departments and to news stories and press releases. Content is under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). The feed carries only a summary, so the sync reads up to four article bodies per source and run from the GOV.UK Content API. Withdrawn pages are skipped, and reading stops at "Notes to editors" or press contacts. Attribution reads "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."
- Global Voices: full text from its RSS under [CC BY 3.0](https://globalvoices.org/about/global-voices-attribution-policy/). The author's name (`news_article_bodies.byline`) and a link to the original appear at the top of the reader, as the licence requires. Captions, embedded quotes and footers are removed. Posts that say they were published first by a partner outlet stay link-only.
- NASA News (`https://www.nasa.gov/feed/`): NASA's all-news feed, under the same NASA-authored full-text rule.
- Not added: The Conversation, whose CC BY-ND guidelines forbid systematic republishing and edits, and UN News, whose terms allow only personal, non-commercial use. VOA Learning English has not updated since March 2025. The NIH, USGS and EPA feeds refuse server requests.
- Additional RSS/Atom: starts disabled and with `MANUAL_PERMISSION_REQUIRED`. The admin must record a reviewed rights basis, select a restrictive policy, and enable it. The server additionally permits only exact hosts in `NEWS_RSS_ALLOWED_HOSTS`. The default list contains NASA hosts. Adding a new publisher requires an explicit environment change and redeploy.
- Original or licensed passages: stored separately with `rights_status` and `rights_basis`. Non-original material requires a source URL. Audio requires a separate rights basis.

The metadata adapter never stores RSS descriptions or arbitrary publisher imagery. Its image adapter stores only approved official-feed image URLs, credits and rights evidence in a separate RLS-protected table. An entry without an eligible image retains its category illustration. NASA and NOAA article-body exceptions store only individually eligible text in the separate rights-controlled table. The provider rejects redirects, IP-address feed hosts, unsupported schemes, large responses, invalid dates, and non-English GDELT items. Requests have a nine-second timeout, bounded retries, and 50-row database batches. URL hashes and a unique database constraint provide idempotency. A failed sync leaves previously imported records intact.

## Production configuration

Vercel server-only environment variables:

```dotenv
SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY=...  # legacy service_role JWT or server-capable secret key
NEWS_SYNC_SECRET=...           # random 32-byte-or-longer secret
NEWS_RSS_ALLOWED_HOSTS=www.nasa.gov,science.nasa.gov,earthobservatory.nasa.gov,www.noaa.gov,oceanservice.noaa.gov,www.nsf.gov
```

The first two must never use the `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix. Production already uses `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` for RLS-protected browser access.

Before applying `202610240009_news_cron.sql`, create two Supabase Vault secrets:

```sql
select vault.create_secret('https://YOUR_PRODUCTION_DOMAIN/api/news/sync', 'news_sync_url');
select vault.create_secret('SAME_VALUE_AS_NEWS_SYNC_SECRET', 'news_sync_secret');
```

The migration enables `pg_net` and `pg_cron`, then schedules `english-skill-lab-news-30m` at `*/30 * * * *`. Cron calls `POST /api/news/sync` with a bearer token read from Vault. The endpoint also supports a same-origin, authenticated admin's manual sync button. Each source has its own minimum interval of at least 30 minutes. Check `cron.job`, `cron.job_run_details`, `net._http_response`, and `public.news_sync_runs` to verify operation. A successful Cron job means the HTTP request was queued; confirm an HTTP 200 and a source-level success row before saying sync works. Never print Vault's decrypted token in logs.

## Automatic questions and illustration search

Migration `202610250010_news_enrichment.sql` schedules `/api/news/enrich` every ten minutes after the sync. It reuses the existing Vault URL and bearer secret. Each bounded run handles at most four missing illustrations and one quiz. The admin's **Tạo câu hỏi & tìm ảnh** button runs one batch immediately. Retries are delayed so failed searches or API calls do not run on every sync.

The image worker searches the [NASA Image and Video Library](https://images.nasa.gov/) and Wikimedia Commons at the same time, with a specific query and then a broader one. Search terms come from the headline and words the article repeats; after a quiz is generated, the AI's `image_query` (the article's concrete visual subject) gives articles still without a picture a second search at no extra AI cost. Both searches are free and need no key. The highest keyword match wins, with NASA imagery preferred on a tie. NASA Library results with third-party creators or copyright/courtesy notices are rejected; accepted ones are credited to NASA and the photographer, under NASA's media guidelines. Commons files must be explicitly marked Public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA; Creative Commons images also require a license URL and creator attribution. Each stored illustration keeps its library page, creator and license. An agency-supplied image always takes priority. If no suitable result is found, the category illustration remains. Migration `202610250016_news_full_text_sources.sql` re-queues articles whose earlier search found nothing.

Each AI set has 20 questions in IELTS Academic Reading format, grouped like a real paper. The groups, in order, are 6 True/False/Not Given, 6 multiple choice (A–D), 4 sentence completion and 4 short-answer questions, with the official IELTS instructions for each group. Migration `202610250017_news_ielts_question_types.sql` adds the question type, written-answer keys and per-attempt totals. Sets are generated only for published, non-sensitive articles stored with at least 2,000 characters (about 330 words) of eligible full text. Every answer must cite a sentence quoted exactly from that text. Written answers are NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS copied from the article; each must appear in the quoted sentence and not in the prompt. A completion sentence has exactly one gap. The server also checks distinct prompts, four distinct choices per multiple-choice question, and that True/False/Not Given statements use more than one answer, preferring a selection that uses all three. `news_submit_auto_quiz` grades written answers in PostgreSQL. Case, punctuation, digit separators and a leading "the/a/an" are ignored (`news_normalize_answer`). The migration removed the earlier ten-question sets so they are regenerated in this format; learners' past scores remain.

Feedback is in Vietnamese (migration `202610250021_news_detailed_explanations.sql`). Each question carries a 2–4 sentence explanation of why the answer is right, naming the paraphrase or synonym it relies on; for NOT GIVEN, it says which detail the article never mentions. Choice questions also carry `option_notes`, one note per option on why it is right or wrong; shuffling keeps each note with its option. The server rejects an explanation or note without Vietnamese letters, and a multiple-choice question without four notes. Notes are kept in the private answer keys and returned only after submission. The learner then sees each option's note, the explanation and the evidence sentence. The migration removed the earlier English-explanation sets so they are regenerated. A live set took about 18 seconds.

Free-tier economy: one request per article produces the questions and the image subject. The model is asked for one spare question per group. A faulty item is replaced by its group's spare, or by a spare from another group, so it does not waste the request. Each group keeps a minimum: 4 TFNG, 4 multiple choice, 2 completion, 2 short answer. The image search and the quiz request run side by side within the route's 60-second limit. A live test produced a valid 20-question set in about 9 seconds. Evidence matching ignores typographic differences in quotes, dashes and spacing. Input is capped at about 12,000 characters, cut at a paragraph. Answer options are shuffled on the server so the correct answer's position carries no hint. An HTTP 429 stops AI work for that run. The article keeps its place in the queue, which resumes after Gemini's suggested delay or, for a daily quota, after the midnight Pacific reset. Optional `GEMINI_MODELS` lists up to three models to try in order; each model has its own free-tier quota. Correct answers stay in a private table; learners submit through an authenticated grading function and their score is saved to their account. AI output should still be reviewed for educational accuracy when possible.

AI generation requires a Gemini API key in Google AI Studio, saved as the server-side `GEMINI_API_KEY` **Production** environment variable in Vercel. Redeploy after adding or changing it. The key must never be committed to Git or prefixed with `NEXT_PUBLIC_`. The worker uses `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, which has a Free tier with project-specific request limits. Check the active limits in Google AI Studio; requests beyond the quota fail and are retried later. Free-tier content may be used to improve Google's products, so review that data policy before enabling scheduled generation. Existing manually authored Learn with News questions remain separate.

Migration `202610250014_news_admin_controls.sql` adds the Daily News controls in `/admin/news/settings`. Admins can pause scheduled syncing or enrichment without removing the Cron jobs, set a daily cap for scheduled AI quiz attempts, and add up to 4,000 characters of editorial guidance to the quiz prompt. The server always keeps the evidence and answer-format requirements. **Try prompt** generates a private, unsaved preview using a selected article with full text; it consumes a Gemini request. The buttons at the top of the admin page still run manually when schedules are paused. **Đồng bộ ngay** bypasses the per-source wait interval; scheduled syncs continue to respect it.

`/admin/news/manual` creates or edits a Daily News item in a dedicated manual source. It requires an HTTPS original URL, title, publisher, category and publication date. Full text is optional and requires a recorded reuse basis; new items enter the review queue and are not shown to learners until published. Editing full text invalidates the previously generated quiz so it can be regenerated from the new evidence. This manual Daily News path is distinct from the editor-authored Learn with News lessons.

## Limits

GDELT availability and the publishers' metadata quality are external. Article-list publication times can sometimes be the time GDELT observed an item rather than its original publication time; the UI displays the supplied time without inventing a replacement. RSS dates are read from `pubDate`, `dc:date`, `published`, or `updated`. Source errors are visible in the admin log and the last successful check is shown to learners. A collection older than two hours displays a stale notice.

Source policy and licensed-content evidence must be reviewed by a responsible editor. RSS availability by itself does not grant full-content reuse. No BBC or Guardian feed is enabled. The seven original sample readings are educational examples, not breaking news.
